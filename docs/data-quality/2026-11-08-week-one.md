## Q4. How far does `expected_arrival` move in the last 120 s?

Final minute: for ids leaving the board within 120 s, the last sighting versus the poll before. Held: time to station fell by less than 20 s between two polls that start within 120 s. Scanned 27 MB (42 MB for the split).

| Line | Final move p50 | p90 | Over 45 s | Held share | Held moving over 45 s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Victoria | 15 s | 61 s | 19.9% | 51.7% | 30.5% |
| Central | 6 s | 57 s | 12.8% | 51.8% | 39.6% |
| Northern | 6 s | 60 s | 16.5% | 56.7% | 49.8% |
| Piccadilly | 7 s | 60 s | 16.5% | 68.2% | 56.4% |
| District | 6 s | 61 s | 22.6% | 67.1% | 57.0% |
| Elizabeth | 0 s | 61 s | 13.9% | 90.5% | 69.4% |
| Windrush, Mildmay | 0 s | 72 to 76 s | 31% to 32% | 95% to 97% | 73% to 74% |
| Liberty, Suffragette | 0 to 38 s | 105 s | 44% to 50% | 98% to 99% | 74% to 76% |

Tube by period: weekday peak 17.2% over 45 s (p50 9 s, p90 60 s), weekday other 17.3%, weekend 17.7%. Held share is 54.6% at peak, 61.1% off peak, 63.5% at weekends. Peak is not worse.

- A held train's estimate moves more than 45 s a minute in 30% to 57% of Tube cases and 70% to 76% of Elizabeth and Overground cases. The 45 s rule cannot follow held trains on any line.
- ADR-002: the trigger is an error over 45 s for most lines. It is 13% to 23% on the Tube, so it is not met. The proxy has no ground truth (it measures revision, not error against the real arrival). Recommendation: keep 60 s. Firm for the Tube; the Overground lines at 30% to 50% are mostly standing-train dwell.
- Stale time to station. `time_to_station` is relative to the prediction's own timestamp, about 40 s older than the poll, and repeats between polls. Victoria id 217 left the Victoria board at the 13:04:25 poll showing 121 s, with expected arrival 13:04:41, 16 s away. A rule of `time_to_station <= 120` drops trains whose last sighting shows 121 to 180 s. Remaining time, `expected_arrival - polled_at`, is the right quantity.

## Recommendations

- D25 (duplicates): keep 15 s, and add a condition that the two predictions share a destination. Close pairs with different destinations are two trains (Central 93%, Elizabeth 66%, Northern 26%). Provisional until the SQL model runs on destinations. No effect on Victoria.
- D26 (linking and arrival): keep the mutual-nearest link and the standing link. Link by vehicle id on enabled lines, which removes the held-train over-count (King's Cross inbound 117% to 89%). Replace `time_to_station <= 120` with `expected_arrival - polled_at <= 120` (about 2.7 points on Victoria). Keep the 45 s collapse. Keep 60 s polling (ADR-002).
- D27 (lines on vehicle id): switch on now for Victoria. Switch on after Monday's check, with `000` and `001` as no id, for Northern, Jubilee, Piccadilly, Central, Bakerloo, District, Hammersmith & City, Circle and Metropolitan. Elizabeth and the Overground lines use trip ids and can be switched on. Not DLR (no ids), not Waterloo & City (termini only), not tram (unmeasurable).
- D28 (boards): keep the platform as the board at core stations (2% platform changes), but pool platforms by vehicle id for Metropolitan and District (14% and 6.5%). Keep the null-direction board separate and out of headways. The staging dedupe key must change first (Q5).
- D31 (same-minute departures): keep counting both. The Canning Town pairs are two trains on two platforms.
- Decision requested outside D25 to D34: change the `stg_arrivals` dedupe key (DLR loses 84% of rows, Tube termini up to 38%). Mark the DLR unmeasured in M4.

Python used the reference implementation unchanged: `infer_arrivals_from_rows(rows, polls, InferenceParams(vehicle_id_lines=frozenset({"victoria"})))`, with `000` and `001` set to null, and for variant C `time_to_station` replaced by `max(0, expected_arrival - polled_at)` in seconds.
